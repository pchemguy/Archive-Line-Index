# Professional writing

## Scope and authority

Apply conventions for reports, proposals, technical correspondence, decision briefs, and other professional texts. Improve purpose, audience fit, recommendations, actions, and commitments. Do not invent organizational positions, promises, approvals, commercial terms, responsibilities, or deadlines. Drafting a message does not authorize sending it.

## Inputs and missing information

Use the source material, intended audience, purpose, requested tone, relevant constraints, and known decision or commitment status. Ask about unresolved commitments before writing them as definite. If the user wants only editing, preserve the existing position and flag material uncertainty.

## Procedure

1. Identify what the reader needs to know, decide, approve, or do. Lead with the main point when genre and context support it; provide background where the reader needs it.
2. Identify genre-specific content using the checks below. Separate facts, analysis, recommendations, requests, and commitments so their status remains clear.
3. Check decision logic and feasibility as supported by supplied material. Are criteria, alternatives, trade-offs, assumptions, costs, timing, and constraints available where needed? Flag missing support rather than create favorable figures.
4. State actions precisely where authorized: actor, action, timing, condition, and decision needed. Retain unknowns or proposed status; do not assign responsibility or approval absent from the source.
5. Adapt register to the audience and relationship. Prefer clear professional language, respectful directness, and concise explanations. Avoid inflated formality, unsupported urgency, or softened obligations that change their meaning.
6. Apply [composition analysis](composition-analysis.md), [structural revision](structural-revision.md), and [language revision](language-revision.md) as needed. For a summary, preserve essential reasoning, factors, conditions, and qualifications, not only the recommendation.
7. Check names, dates, amounts, attachments mentioned, and references against supplied material. Evidence mode governs external verification. Flag absent attachments without assuming they do not exist elsewhere.
8. Use [revision check](revision-check.md) to compare commitments, requests, terms, and authorial position before delivery.

## Genre checks

| Form | Check |
|---|---|
| Report | Clear question/purpose, supported findings, implications, limits, and recommendations distinguished from findings. |
| Proposal | Need, proposed approach, scope/exclusions, value, assumptions, dependencies, and supplied resource/commercial terms; status is explicit. |
| Decision brief | Decision required, options and criteria, recommendation basis, trade-offs, uncertainty, and next action. |
| Correspondence | Informative subject where relevant, clear purpose, necessary context, appropriate request or response, and unambiguous next step. |
| Executive summary | Main result and practical implications, with indispensable support and qualifications retained at the required length. |

## Outputs and examples

Deliver the requested clean draft, annotated revision, or review. For correspondence, provide subject and body separately when useful. If only a draft is requested, do not send, schedule, or publish it.

"Could deliver in June if testing passes" must not become "We guarantee June delivery". A cost proposal without approved prices should retain proposed terms or placeholders. A shorter memo must preserve the assumptions and numerical factors that explain its recommendation. A formal request may be direct without inventing authority or an ultimatum.

If a request requires specialized legal, medical, or financial conclusions beyond available support, distinguish editing from substantive advice and use evidence work within authorized scope; do not add unsupported conclusions to the document.

## Completion checks

Purpose and requested action are clear, genre needs are met, and support and uncertainty are proportionate. Names, terms, commitments, and decision status match supplied material. No message was sent or publication implied by the drafting workflow.
