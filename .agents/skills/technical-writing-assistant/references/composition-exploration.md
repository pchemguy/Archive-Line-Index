# Composition exploration

## Scope and authority

Explore alternative framing, organization, emphasis, and explanatory or argumentative sequences before major restructuring or drafting. Use when the material admits materially different approaches or the current composition does not serve its purpose. Skip for a clear local repair. Recommend approaches without manufacturing support or silently choosing a new authorial position.

## Inputs and missing information

Use the brief, notes or draft, known evidence, and findings from prior review. Identify indispensable content and unresolved substantive choices. If purpose or audience is unclear enough to change the organizing principle, use [writing brief](writing-brief.md) or ask a focused question.

## Procedure

1. Identify the communication problem: what must readers understand, decide, or do? Distinguish this from the author's list of topics.
2. Separate central claims, necessary context, supporting explanation, qualifications, and ancillary detail. Mark missing support and unconfirmed ideas.
3. Identify plausible organizing principles from the material: reader questions, problem and response, chronological process, concept dependencies, comparison, claim and support, or another context-appropriate structure. These are examples, not an exhaustive catalogue.
4. Develop two or three materially distinct alternatives when alternatives add value. Show a short outline or paragraph sequence for each, including what would be foregrounded, combined, deferred, or omitted. Do not create near-identical options for appearance.
5. Compare trade-offs against audience, purpose, evidence, length, genre, and reader burden. Explain information loss or substantive implications. An elegant arrangement cannot rescue unsupported claims.
6. Recommend an approach with reasons. Proceed within authorized structural scope when the choice is editorial and clear. Seek an author decision when alternatives imply different conclusions, positions, or commitments; continue unaffected work.
7. Pass the selected organization and constraints to [structural revision](structural-revision.md) or [draft development](draft-development.md). For an outline or map as a standalone artifact, use [outline context exploration](outline-context-exploration.md) and [outline generation](outline-generation.md).

## Outputs

Return alternatives, their actual organizing differences, trade-offs, a recommendation, and unresolved decisions. Include an outline only at the detail necessary to assess the approach. Mark proposed content and needed evidence separately from supplied ideas.

## Examples and failure handling

- A technical decision memo can lead with the recommendation and then compare options, or lead with unresolved requirements before reaching a conditional recommendation. Which is better depends on whether the decision is ready.
- A scientific explanation can introduce a mechanism before an example or use the example to motivate the mechanism. Neither permits adding an experimental finding absent from the sources.
- If the supplied material supports only one defensible conclusion, do not offer an unsupported "stronger" version as an equivalent compositional alternative.
- If the author requests only alternatives, deliver them without rewriting the full document. If no meaningful alternative improves the text, explain that rather than force options.

## Completion checks

Alternatives differ in meaningful composition, preserve indispensable content, and expose support needs and substantive consequences. The recommendation is justified by the brief. The selected approach can guide the next workflow without another generic intake.
