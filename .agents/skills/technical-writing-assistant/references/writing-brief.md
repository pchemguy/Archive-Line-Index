# Writing brief

## Scope and authority

Use to resolve purpose, audience, genre, constraints, or editing authority before work that depends on them. Skip a separate intake for a self-contained correction. Define the task without rewriting the material or imposing a questionnaire.

## Inputs and missing information

Read the request, supplied text and sources, established preferences, intended use, and requested output. Separate explicit instructions from inferred defaults. If the text is absent, ask for the relevant material; do not reconstruct it from a title. If only part is supplied, scope the work to that part.

## Procedure

1. Extract the objective: diagnosis, revision, development, alternatives, research, outline/map work, or a combination. Preserve requested exclusions, word limits, and output format.
2. Identify the audience's knowledge and the action or understanding the text should support. Infer ordinary details when low risk; state assumptions that materially affect the result.
3. Establish genre, dialect, register, and applicable house style. User instructions govern defaults. Preserve the language unless translation is requested.
4. Identify governing source material and conflicts. A draft shows what the author wrote; it does not automatically establish factual correctness. Do not silently choose between conflicting authoritative instructions.
5. Establish revision authority from the request. Ordinary editing permits wording repairs; restructuring permits organization changes; argument critique permits proposals. Broad improvement does not authorize inventing a position, commitment, or finding.
6. Ask only questions whose answers change the work materially. Combine related uncertainties in a short question when practical. Continue independent work while a consequential answer is pending; do not assume an answer for dependent work.
7. If evidence work is relevant, use [the manager's mode-selection rule](manager.md#evidence-modes-and-selection). Do not ask about research merely to correct punctuation.
8. Return or retain a concise brief. For substantial work, record purpose, audience, material, authority, constraints, evidence mode, and deliverables. For a paragraph edit, a sentence of assumptions may suffice.

## Outputs and handoffs

Supply a usable brief, unresolved decisions, and the basis of important assumptions. Pass it to the selected workflow without requiring a new approval at every handoff. Use [material assessment](material-assessment.md) for uncertain readiness and [outline context exploration](outline-context-exploration.md) for representation-specific choices.

## Examples and failure handling

- "Polish this paragraph for a technical report, preserving the numbers" already defines sufficient authority. Edit the paragraph; retain numerical factors and qualifications. Do not ask for a full project history.
- "Turn these preliminary findings into a press statement" may leave publication status and authorized claims unresolved. Ask about those substantive choices while assessing structure; do not announce confirmed results.
- Conflicting US/UK house-style instructions require clarification if applicable precedence cannot be established. Minor missing preferences may be handled with consistent existing usage.

## Completion checks

The brief reflects the actual request, repeats no answered question, distinguishes assumptions from instructions, and identifies every unresolved decision that blocks dependent work. The selected scope and deliverable are clear enough to proceed.
