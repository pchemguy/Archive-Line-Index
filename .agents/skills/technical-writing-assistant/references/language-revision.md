# Language revision

## Scope and authority

Improve mechanics, clarity, precision, concision, register, collocations, sentence construction, pronoun references, and voice while preserving substantive meaning. Use independently for a passage or within broader revision. Do not silently change claims, certainty, scope, causality, position, obligations, or commitments.

## Inputs and missing information

Use the text, requested dialect/style, audience, revision depth, and protected expressions. Prefer existing consistent usage when minor preferences are unspecified. Resolve materially ambiguous meaning before dependent edits; do not select a convenient interpretation just to produce fluent prose. Apply [style guidance](style-guidelines.md) selectively.

## Procedure

1. Read for meaning before editing. Identify subject, action, quantities, conditions, logical relations, and qualifiers. Preserve quotations, code, identifiers, equations, citation locators, and standardized expressions unless their correction is authorized.
2. Correct grammar, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, agreement, and modifier attachment according to the selected dialect. Retain appropriate fragments in headings, labels, tables, and concise lists.
3. Clarify agency, references, information order, and sentence boundaries. Prefer active voice where helpful; retain purposeful passive constructions. If the actor is unknown, do not invent one.
4. Replace vague or unnatural wording with precise, conventional expressions without erasing field-specific usage. Check collocations and commonly confused words. Keep established technical terms stable.
5. Remove redundancy and empty phrasing. Preserve essential reasoning, numerical factors, evidence, conditions, and caveats. Do not turn "may" into "will", association into causation, or advice into a requirement to make a sentence stronger.
6. Improve sentence variety and readability based on function, not numerical quotas. Split overload where relations remain clear; combine fragments where they develop one idea. Larger changes belong to [structural revision](structural-revision.md).
7. Adjust register and contractions to audience and instructions. Default to clear professional language; do not inflate ordinary words into ornate formality.
8. Compare each material edit with the source and check surrounding flow. Separate meaning-changing proposals and unresolved ambiguities from clean wording repairs. Use [terminology consistency](terminology-consistency.md) for recurring terms and [revision check](revision-check.md) for the final comparison.

## Outputs

Deliver clean or annotated text as requested. Include material uncertainties and substantive proposals when relevant; do not narrate every punctuation change. A review-only request receives findings or suggested edits rather than an automatic full rewrite.

## Examples and failure handling

- "The device may reduce losses under dry conditions" must not become "The device reduces losses". Retain uncertainty and conditions even in a concise revision.
- "After testing the sample, the error was corrected" has unclear agency. If the source does not identify the tester, propose a clarification rather than invent a person.
- "This increased it" may require identifying both references from context. If context cannot resolve them, flag the ambiguity.
- "The process requires calibration, validating sensors, and logs" needs parallel structure; preserve whether logging is an action or an artifact requirement.
- A quotation containing an error remains a quotation. Flag it or correct its surrounding description; do not silently rewrite the quoted words.

## Completion checks

Grammar and relevant style criteria are consistent. Meaning, logical relationships, conditions, essential support, and protected material survive. Edits fit the requested voice and scope. Substantive changes or unresolved interpretations are visible.
