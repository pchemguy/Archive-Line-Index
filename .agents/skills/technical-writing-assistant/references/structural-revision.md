# Structural revision

## Scope and authority

Implement authorized changes to organization, grouping, hierarchy, paragraph boundaries, sequence, headings, transitions, and placement of supporting material. Use after diagnosis or for a clear restructuring request. Rearrange and develop existing content without inventing facts or silently changing the argument.

## Inputs and missing information

Use the source text, brief, structural findings or selected approach, protected material, and indispensable content. For extensive work, retain a source version or content inventory so omissions can be checked. If source authority conflicts, flag it before dependent revision.

## Procedure

1. Identify each unit's actual purpose and the required document trajectory. Use [composition analysis](composition-analysis.md) for unclear defects and [composition exploration](composition-exploration.md) when competing approaches need comparison.
2. Select authorized repairs. Preserve essential reasoning, quantities, caveats, citations, examples, and requirements; mark proposed removals that would change substance. For diagnosed size/detail problems, trim or consolidate redundant treatment, redistribute detail, or develop supported essentials as needed; retain useful recurrence and flag unavailable substance.
3. Reorder and group content around meaningful purposes. Place definitions and prerequisites before use; keep evidence and qualifications near the claims they support. Preserve procedural dependencies and cross-references.
4. Combine fragments that develop one controlling idea. Split overloaded units where distinct purposes warrant boundaries. After splitting, ensure adequate development rather than accidental isolated fragments. Prefer a sound topic sentence in each expository paragraph; retain purposeful brief warnings, transitions, and other justified exceptions without padding them.
5. Revise headings, topic sentences, lead-ins, and transitions to match the new content. Express the actual relation: contrast, sequence, qualification, explanation, or inference. Do not create a causal link or missing premise to smooth a move.
6. Relocate lists, figures, equations, examples, or quotations with their introductions and interpretation. Update references when locations change; preserve identifiers that must remain stable.
7. Develop connective explanation only within supported meaning. Flag a substantive gap or hand it to [draft development](draft-development.md), [argument review](argument-review.md), or [evidence review](evidence-review.md) as needed.
8. Compare the revised structure with the source inventory. Recheck local flow after moves and global continuity after all changes. Use [language revision](language-revision.md) for sentence polish and [revision check](revision-check.md) for final comparison.

## Outputs

Return the requested clean or annotated revision, significant structural changes when useful, and unresolved substantive gaps. In a review-only task, provide recommendations instead of implementation. Do not make every paragraph move an approval gate; ask when the move entails an unresolved substantive decision.

## Examples and failure handling

- A paragraph describes limitations, then introduces a new method. Split at the purpose change and give the method paragraph an accurate topic sentence; retain the limitations near the claims they qualify.
- Moving "under simulated loads" away from a performance result can broaden the claim. Keep the condition with the result even if the new paragraph would read more smoothly without it.
- A procedure's verification step cannot precede its setup merely because the introduction mentions verification first. Preserve operational order.
- If a requested outline requires a result the source never supplies, flag the missing content rather than write a plausible conclusion.

## Completion checks

Arrangement serves the brief, unit boundaries are purposeful, and headings/topic sentences match content. Transitions express real relationships. No required content, qualification, numerical factor, citation, or dependency was lost or reassigned misleadingly. Remaining substantive gaps are explicit.
